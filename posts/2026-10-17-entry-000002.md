My departure draws near, and a formidable multitude of preparations remains to be undertaken. Naturally, I have accomplished nothing.

First, provisions must be secured for The Cat, whose continued existence during my absence is a matter of considerable importance, particularly to himself. Fortunately, I have enlisted the services of a nearby devotee of felines, a person of trustworthy disposition and, I am assured, sufficient affection to prevent the creature from perishing through neglect. Thus, one obligation may be considered settled, though the provisions themselves remain conspicuously absent.

My bottomless bag, an indispensable article whose present condition renders its title a cruel mockery, has yet to be repaired. I must deliver it to a craftsman before my departure, lest I be forced to carry my possessions in the barbarous fashion of ordinary travelers.

Then there is the matter of film. My photographic ambitions are considerable, and I should regard it as a personal affront to posterity were some magnificent spectacle to present itself while my camera stood empty.

Most troubling of all, I have not packed a single article. My house, too, remains in a condition that demands attention, for I should prefer to return to a civilized dwelling rather than the aftermath of some minor catastrophe.

Thus I find myself besieged by obligations, each perfectly reasonable in isolation, yet collectively forming a conspiracy against my leisure.

Still, I possess two advantages: a quantity of time, rapidly diminishing, and an inexhaustible capacity for indecision.

Of these, the latter has proven by far the more dependable.
