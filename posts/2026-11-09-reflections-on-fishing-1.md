I've been fishing these waters and others for many a year, and suffered enough triumphs and humiliations to know that a fish is a deceitful little bastard. Still, there is nothing quite like casting a line across a quiet lake, where the water sits still as a dead wizard's bath.

We had ventured north for a bit of cave exploration when I spotted Pine Lake on a map, not far from our cabin. Figured I'd bring along my rod, reel, and a modest box of tackle, on the off chance fortune felt generous.

Well, I'll be damned if I didn't hook a rainbow trout within seconds of reaching the water! A little fellow, sure, but handsome enough, and destined to make a fine supper. I strung him up straightaway, reckoning I'd soon have enough fish to feed a minor kingdom.

That was my first mistake.

For the next several hours, I cast my line with the patience of a saint and the vocabulary of a condemned highwayman. Not another nibble. Not a twitch. Those fish had evidently convened some secret council and declared my hooks an offense against their sovereign dignity.

So I departed with one small trout, a belly full of indignation, and the satisfaction of knowing the little bastard tasted considerably better than his friends had behaved.
