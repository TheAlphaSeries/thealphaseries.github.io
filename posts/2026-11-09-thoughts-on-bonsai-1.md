date: 2026-11-09

Last year, I undertook the cultivation of a modest collection of bonsai, those miniature trees whose survival depends upon patience, careful attention, and, as I have recently discovered, water.

For a time, my efforts proved successful. New growth appeared, and I began to regard myself as a horticulturist of uncommon promise.

Then came China.

In the frenzy of preparing for my expedition, I neglected to arrange for the trees' care during my absence. Upon my return, I discovered that my entire collection had perished. Not one possessed the ingenuity to survive independently.

The tragedy was particularly absurd, as I am a member of the San Francisco Bonsai Club, an organization filled with individuals who might gladly have assisted. Alas, time was short, my attention divided, and laziness had conquered good judgment.

Thus, through negligence and the unreasonable dependence of plants upon water, my miniature forest became a miniature graveyard.

I intend to acquire two or three new specimens, fortified by experience and, ideally, a more reliable watering arrangement.
