In the middle of the bay lies an island that was not there until men wanted one, and named, with the optimism of its builders, for a treasure no one has yet found. Its western shore faces the city squarely across the water, and the savants of the dark box agree that there is no better place from which to take the whole of it at once.

The hour is everything. Too early and the towers are merely buildings; too late and they are merely lights. Between the two there is a quarter of an hour in which they are both, and the wind off the water, which is constant and personal, does its best to spoil the plate. A weighted stand is advised. So is a look at the sky before setting out, the fog having views of its own.
